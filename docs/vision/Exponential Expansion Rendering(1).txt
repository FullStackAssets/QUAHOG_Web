VISUALS — RENDERING AND LIGHTING

Real-Time Global Illumination Ecosystem

· Cascaded lightmap baking with dynamic time-of-day interpolation: Pre-bake 8-12 lighting states per architectural zone, then blend between them in real-time as the sun moves—dawn's pink wash bleeding into noon's hard clarity bleeding into golden hour's amber embrace, all without a single hitch on mobile silicon
· Screen-space reflections with temporal accumulation and ray-marched fallbacks: Wet asphalt mirrors streetlights with a one-frame delay that's imperceptible; puddles show inverted neon signs; car paint reflects the sky and nearby buildings with Fresnel-correct intensity that shifts as you circle the vehicle
· Irradiance probe grids per interior cell: Every shop, apartment, and bar has its own volumetric probe lattice—light from a single flickering fluorescent tube bounces off counters, stains walls with sickly green, and dies in corners the way real light dies
· Cascaded voxel cone tracing for large-scale ambient occlusion: Buildings cast soft shadows on each other; alleyways darken naturally; overhangs create pools of shade that shift minute by minute
· Per-object dynamic light lists with forward+ rendering: Hundreds of small light sources (streetlamps, neon signs, car headlights, muzzle flashes, cigarette tips) all contribute simultaneously without a visible light-count cliff
· Subsurface scattering on skin, foliage, and thin fabrics: Fingers glow red when backlit by sunset; leaves transmit green-gold light; curtains diffuse streetlight into warm interior glow
· Precomputed atmospheric scattering with Rayleigh and Mie approximation: The sky isn't a gradient—it's a physical simulation. Sunsets bloom orange at the horizon and fade to deep indigo at zenith; the moon has phase-correct illumination; stars appear based on light pollution per district
· Reflection probes with parallax correction and roughness-aware mip selection: Chrome bumpers, shop windows, and wet sidewalks all reflect the correct part of the world at the correct blur level

Dynamic Time-of-Day Amplified

· 24-hour continuous cycle with 1440 discrete lighting evaluation points: Every minute has its own sun angle, color temperature, shadow length, and ambient intensity—no visible stepping
· Golden hour lengthens shadows to 15x object height, turns glass facades into mirrors of fire, and bathes everything in a warmth that makes the same street look like a different world
· Blue hour (the 20 minutes after sunset) drops ambient to 5% and lets artificial lights take over—streetlamps flicker on in sequence, windows glow, neon ignites, and the city becomes a different organism
· Night gameplay visibility system: Unlit alleys are genuinely dark—you need headlights, you need to stick to lit streets, you need to think about light. Flashlights have battery. Car high-beams reveal pedestrians at 50m but blind oncoming traffic. Criminals use darkness. Police searchlights sweep.
· Shadow-casting moonlight with cloud interference: When clouds pass, moon-shadows soften and disappear, then sharpen again—a slow strobe that changes tactical visibility
· Artificial light scheduling per building type: Offices go dark at 6pm, bars light up at 8pm, 24-hour shops stay fluorescent all night, residential windows flicker with TV blue
· Seasonal daylight variation (if seasons implemented): December sunsets at 4:30pm, June at 9pm—the same street, radically different mood
· Weather-time interaction: Overcast days compress dynamic range and flatten shadows; clear days maximize contrast; fog at dawn burns off by 10am; storm clouds darken noon to twilight

Volumetric Atmosphere

· Ray-marched volumetric fog with temporal reprojection: Fog has density—you can see it thicken in valleys, thin on hilltops, and pool in basements
· God rays computed per-light-source with screen-space scattering: Sunbeams through broken windows, through tree canopies, through chain-link fences—each beam a separate light shaft with correct falloff
· Light shafts through architecture: Cathedral-height warehouses have visible light columns; stairwells have angular beams; blinds cast striped shadows that move with the sun
· Height fog with ground-hugging behavior: Morning mist clings to the bayou at ankle height; exhaust hangs in still air; smoke from fires pools before rising
· Participating media for headlights: Night fog makes every car a visible cone of light—you see cars coming around corners before you see the cars
· Volumetric clouds with parallax and light scattering: Not a skybox—actual 3D cloud layers that cast moving shadows on the ground and glow at their edges when backlit
· Localized volumetric effects: Steam vents in sidewalks, exhaust from idling trucks, smoke from chimneys, dust motes in sunbeams—each a small volumetric system with its own density falloff
· Underwater volumetric fog with depth-based color absorption: Red disappears first, then orange, then yellow—the deeper you dive, the bluer and darker the world becomes

Wet Surface Systems Amplified

· Dynamic wetness accumulation with runoff simulation: Rain doesn't just "make things wet"—water accumulates on surfaces, pools in low areas, flows downhill, and drains into gutters
· Puddle formation based on surface geometry: Depressions fill first; crowned roads shed water to edges; flat roofs become mirrors
· Tire spray and pedestrian splash: Cars kick up rooster tails; footsteps create ripple rings; bicycles leave wet tracks that fade over minutes
· Rain-slicked surfaces with anisotropic reflections: Wet asphalt reflects light directionally—headlights smear into long streaks; streetlights become vertical columns of orange
· Car paint water beading with droplet-level normal perturbation: Fresh wax beads water into spheres; neglected paint sheets water flat; driving speed blows droplets backward across the surface
· Drying simulation with wind and sun exposure: North-facing surfaces stay wet longer; shaded areas dry slowly; wind accelerates evaporation; puddles shrink from edges inward
· Wet fabric on characters: Rain darkens clothing, changes its specular response, adds weight to animation (subtle slump in shoulders, slower movements)
· Interior wetness tracking: Rain blown through open windows wets sills; wet shoes leave prints on dry floors that evaporate over time

HDR and Color Science

· 16-bit internal render target with ACES filmic tone mapping: Highlights roll off naturally—sun glints on chrome don't clip to white; deep shadows retain detail
· Per-district color grading LUTs: The tourist district is saturated and warm; the industrial zone is desaturated and cold; the bayou is green-shifted and humid; the nightlife strip is magenta-cyan neon
· Per-time-of-day grading variation: Same district, different hour—noon is neutral, golden hour is warm, night is crushed blacks with lifted midtones for that cinematic "day for night" feel
· Bloom with energy conservation and lens dirt: Bright lights bloom with physically-plausible intensity falloff; camera lens accumulates dirt, fingerprints, and rain droplets that scatter light
· Chromatic aberration on high-contrast edges: Subtle at rest, intensifying during action—impacts, explosions, and crashes briefly split RGB channels for visceral punch
· Vignetting with dynamic intensity: Wide FOV during chases darkens edges (speed sensation); cutscenes use heavy vignette for intimacy; photo mode has adjustable vignette
· Film grain with per-scene intensity: Gritty missions get heavier grain; clean scenes get almost none; grain pattern is animated and resolution-independent
· Color grading driven by narrative state: Story acts have different color palettes—Act 1 is bright and hopeful, Act 3 is desaturated and ominous

GAMEPLAY — DRIVING AND PHYSICS

Vehicle Handling Exponential

· Per-vehicle physics profiles: Every vehicle has unique mass, center of gravity, suspension, grip, and power delivery
· Weight transfer simulation: Accelerating squats the rear; braking dives the front; cornering leans outside
· Suspension simulation: Individual wheel suspension with spring rates, damping, and travel
· Tire model with slip angles: Tires grip until they don't—understeer, oversteer, and drift all emerge naturally
· Drivetrain simulation: FWD, RWD, AWD—each with distinct handling characteristics
· Transmission simulation: Gear ratios, shift points, clutch behavior—manual and automatic
· Engine simulation: Power curves, turbo lag, rev limiting—each engine has character
· Brake simulation: Brake fade, ABS, handbrake—all with proper behavior
· Damage affects handling: Bent frames pull; blown tires drag; broken suspension wobbles
· Surface interaction: Different grip on asphalt, concrete, dirt, grass, sand, snow, ice, water
· Weather affects grip: Rain reduces grip; snow reduces it more; ice is nearly zero
· Aerodynamics: Downforce at speed, drag limiting top speed, draft effects

Vehicle Types and Physics

· Cars with distinct classes: Compacts, sedans, sports, muscle, SUVs, trucks—each with appropriate physics
· Motorcycles with lean physics: Countersteering, wheelies, stoppies—all physics-driven
· Boats with buoyancy and planing: Displacement hulls vs. planing hulls; wake generation; water resistance
· Aircraft with lift and drag: Fixed-wing and rotary-wing; stall behavior; wind effects
· Bicycles with pedal physics: Human-powered; stamina-limited; different handling
· Special vehicles: Tanks, forklifts, buses, fire trucks—each with unique controls and physics
· Vehicle customization: Paint, wheels, body kits, performance upgrades, armor—all affect appearance and performance

Dynamic Road Conditions

· Wet grip reduction: Rain reduces grip by 30-50% depending on intensity; puddles cause hydroplaning
· Snow and ice physics: Snow reduces grip and increases stopping distance; ice is nearly frictionless
· Mud and off-road: Soft surfaces slow vehicles and affect handling
· Road damage: Potholes, cracks, and debris affect handling
· Dynamic conditions: Rain starts and stops; snow accumulates and melts; roads dry over time
· Tire type matters: Summer tires vs. all-season vs. winter—each performs differently in conditions
· Visibility affects driving: Fog, rain, and snow reduce visibility; headlights help but don't eliminate the problem

· Texture streaming with mip bias: Lower resolution textures at distance; higher up close

· Budget-based rendering: Frame time budget allocated to different systems; they scale to fit

This is not a game with features bolted on. This is a living world simulator where every system feeds every other system. The goal is not to check boxes. The goal is to create a place that feels real—a place you can get lost in, a place that surprises you, a place that generates stories you'll tell for years.

Every expansion above serves that goal. Every system is in conversation with every other system. The result is not a list of features—it's a world.
